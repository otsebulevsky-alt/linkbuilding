** 📊 Fact of the Week
------------------------------------------------------------

A post from Lily Ray (https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7434978494960500736/?commentUrn=urn%3Ali%3Acomment%3A&utm_source=BuzzStream+Mailer&utm_campaign=b9726624e4-EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_03_05_26&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_0e868c52d2-b9726624e4-1449713387(activity%3A7434978494960500736%2C7435015801948078099)&dashCommentUrn=urn%3Ali%3Afsd_comment%3A(7435015801948078099%2Curn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7434978494960500736)) highlighted that some AI Search companies are paying people to post promotional messages claiming that GEO is the future and SEO is dead.

🧐 Why should you care?

Really, it’s just another reminder that what you see out there isn’t always the truth.

💡 What should you do about it?

Be mindful of the advice you hear online. Just because someone says something doesn’t make it true. Look for data to support your choices and decisions.

Also, don’t run to the first shiny tool or tactic that looks appealing. Remember, most people on social only share the good stuff, never the bad.

What works for one site rarely works for every site.


** 🐝 Tip of the Week
------------------------------------------------------------

Have you checked out Pinterest Trends (https://trends.pinterest.com/?utm_source=BuzzStream+Mailer&utm_campaign=b9726624e4-EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_03_05_26&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_0e868c52d2-b9726624e4-1449713387) yet?

If not, you are missing out on potential content and reactive PR ideas.

For reactive PR, trends are a fantastic way to get your brand or experts in front of journalists and writers covering similar stories.

Pitches like: “‘Hot Pot Ideas’ is trending on Pinterest and we wanted to share our expert chef’s do’s and don’t’s,” along with some custom tips for a story, can get you quick features in certain publications.

This is especially common in some UK publications. A search for “food + expert reveals” uncovered this piece from DailyMail (https://www.dailymail.co.uk/lifestyle/food-drink/article-15505435/From-reheating-rice-scrambled-egg-prep-food-expert-reveals-13-common-kitchen-myths-ignore.html?utm_source=BuzzStream+Mailer&utm_campaign=b9726624e4-EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_03_05_26&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_0e868c52d2-b9726624e4-1449713387) .

Or here is a Thanksgiving-themes experts piece from Fox News (https://www.foxnews.com/food-drink/stop-carb-overload-experts-expose-hidden-reason-big-meals-backfire-how-protein-flips-switch?utm_source=BuzzStream+Mailer&utm_campaign=b9726624e4-EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_03_05_26&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_0e868c52d2-b9726624e4-1449713387) .

The trick is to frame the pitch alongside the trend so the journalist understands it is timely.

Trending item + expert tip = more likelihood of coverage.

But remember, these journalists have access to Pinterest too. That’s where the tips come in. They provide extra value that the journalist might not readily be able to get.

Extra points if you can also include proprietary data.

For example, if “hot pot ideas” is trending and your brand offers cooking classes, there may also be proprietary data showing that hot pot classes are up 50%.

Then you have something even better.
