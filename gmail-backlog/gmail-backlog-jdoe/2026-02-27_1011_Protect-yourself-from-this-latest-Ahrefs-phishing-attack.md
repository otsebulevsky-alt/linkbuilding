Hi Feruza, 
 We're writing to you to warn about an active phishing attack targeting Ahrefs users and other platform subscribers. 
 What's happening 
 Scammers are using paid Google Ads to place fake results that look convincingly like real platforms on the top of search results:

 [Image "image.png?expires=1790857026&amp;signature=4305cca2e3ef874dce5c565b491172df67c84096b98cc595e0403848c21ceb23&amp;req=diEmFsp4mINXUfMW2XS%2FiZpOf6BG8vwe35KqYDjlkJdgsee%2Bjpg%3D%0A"] If you do, and grant the requested permissions, the attacker gains access to your Google Ads account. Confirmed victims have lost over $30,000 each from their accounts getting drained this way. 
 How to protect yourself right now 
 1. Always go directly to ahrefs.com by typing it into your browser instead of clicking a search result or ad. 
2. Check your address bar every time you log in. The real site will always show `ahrefs.com`. If website domain that you're on looks even slightly off, close that page. 
3. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) with a security key on both your Google account and Ahrefs account. This is your strongest defense against credential theft. 
4. Check before you approve Google permission requests from unfamiliar apps. If a pop-up asks for access and you're not sure why, decline it. 
 Why is this so hard to stop? 
 Unfortunately, blocking this kind of attack isn't as simple as it sounds. We've written a detailed explanation of why, and what we're doing about it on our engineering blog. ​ If you believe your account has been compromised, please contact our support team immediately at support@ahrefs.com. ​ Stay safe, The Ahrefs Team
